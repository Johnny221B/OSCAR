01 / THE IDEA

Stochasticity
is not enough.

Preserving a trajectory’s marginal distribution does not guarantee an informative group of samples.

Our starting point was a question raised by reinforcement learning for visual generation. Methods such as Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO brought group-relative optimization to generative models, using stochastic sampling to support exploration. Flow-GRPO, in particular, constructs an SDE that preserves the original flow’s time marginals. But distribution-preserving stochasticity leaves another question open: how much meaningful diversity does a finite group actually contain?

While experimenting with Flow-GRPO, we noticed that different rollouts could still produce semantically similar candidates. This matters for group-relative learning: equal rewards provide no reward-based preference signal, while small reward differences between redundant candidates may offer little reliable evidence about alternative generation strategies.

This observation motivated the question behind OSCAR: can we make a fixed set of trajectories explore complementary semantic outcomes while protecting generation quality? Rather than relying on noise alone, OSCAR introduces set-level feature-space guidance and orthogonal control to encourage trajectories to spread.

The RL observation motivated this work; the experiments presented here evaluate generation diversity, quality and alignment, rather than downstream RL training gains.

Read the abstract +

Flow-based text-to-image models follow deterministic trajectories, making it costly to explore diverse modes under limited sampling budgets. We present a training-free, inference-time control mechanism that makes the flow itself diversity-aware. Guidance encourages lateral spread in feature space and introduces time-scheduled stochastic perturbations orthogonal to the generation flow. Across the paper’s text-to-image settings, OSCAR improves set-level diversity while maintaining competitive image quality and prompt alignment.

Scope & limitations +

OSCAR operates on jointly active sets of trajectories and is not designed for a strict one-image-per-prompt setting. Endpoint feature extraction and diversity-gradient computation add inference overhead relative to the original sampler. Results depend on the model, prompts and evaluation setting; see the paper’s limitations and cross-backbone results.
